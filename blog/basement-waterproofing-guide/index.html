Basement Water Prevention Tips in Scottsdale, AZ

Home / Blog / How to Waterproof Your Basement in Scottsdale, AZ

A dry basement protects your home's structure, the air you breathe indoors, and what the property is worth. Even so, plenty of Scottsdale, AZ houses have wet basements, with problems that run from a little seepage up to a full flood. Our team at Scottsdale Restoration Co put this guide together to walk Scottsdale homeowners through the common sources of basement water and the methods that work against them.

What Makes a Basement Wet

Water gets into basements in several ways, and knowing the source is key to picking the right fix. Hydrostatic pressure builds when saturated soil around your foundation pushes water through cracks, joints, and even porous concrete itself. Foundation walls can also crack under the lateral push of expanding clay, which gives water a way inside. Surface water from poor grading, clogged gutters, or weak drainage runs against your foundation and finds a way in. A basement can also be damp without any leak at all, because humid warm air condenses on cool walls and floors.

Outside Fixes for a Wet Basement

For lasting results, the best approach is to stop water on the exterior side of the foundation. A crew excavates along the foundation, puts a waterproof membrane coating on the wall, and then installs new exterior drain tile or upgrades what is there. It is the most expensive approach, but it addresses the problem at its source and gives the most complete protection. Regrading is also worth the money, because ground that falls 1 inch or more per foot across the first 6 feet away from the foundation carries water off.

Inside Fixes That Manage Water

With an interior system, the goal is to collect and move water after it enters, not to block it outside. Water that reaches the basement perimeter is caught by an interior French drain, which carries it to the sump pump to be discharged. A sump pump, preferably one backed up by a battery, handles the water reliably when storms hit or the power goes out. Sealants and waterproof coatings applied inside help with light seepage and efflorescence, yet they are no substitute for real drainage if a lot of water is coming in.

Sump Pump Care and Testing

For Scottsdale homeowners with sump pumps, proper maintenance is essential. Test your sump pump quarterly by pouring water into the pit until the pump turns on. Listen for unusual sounds that could point to mechanical problems. Because outages are when basement flooding is most common, a battery backup is worth installing. For more protection still, think about a second, water-driven pump. Check that discharged water lands a minimum of 6 feet from the house and that a check valve on the line stops backflow.
