Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Scottsdale Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Sort Save From Remove

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Mold Inside Cavities

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.
